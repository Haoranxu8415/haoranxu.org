Visual Dark Patterns & AI Decision-Making

It started with Information Bubbles. Building that project made me think seriously, for the first time, about the relationship between algorithms and attention — how platforms quietly make choices on your behalf, through design, through sequencing, through what they choose to show you first.

Over the winter break, I found myself thinking one layer deeper. AI shopping agents are now capable of browsing products and generating purchase recommendations on behalf of consumers. These agents are multimodal — they receive text and images simultaneously, just like a human shopper would. That means they are exposed to the same visual tactics that e-commerce platforms have spent years optimising for human psychology: inflated strikethrough prices, countdown timers, artificial scarcity signals.

Existing research has shown that text-based price anchoring can influence large language model outputs. But does the visual presentation of that information, independent of its content, produce an additional effect? That question, as far as I could tell, remained unanswered.

The study tested five models across 4,800 API calls, collecting 4,619 valid observations after cleaning. The core finding was consistent across all eight products and all five models: visual dark patterns suppressed model evaluations in every case. Defensive prompting partially worked — effective on deal scores in most cases, but largely ineffective on recommendation scores. A notable cultural divergence emerged between Western and Chinese models under dark-pattern conditions. One model stood as the sole exception, producing a positive effect in the opposite direction.

The preprint and dataset are available at Zenodo. The paper is currently being prepared for submission to an academic journal.
